\IEEEPARstart{C}{ombinatorial} optimization problems (COPs), such as the Traveling Salesman Problem (\TSP{}) and the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (\CVRP{}), have broad applications in logistics, transportation, and network design~\cite{TSPAPP}. As NP-hard problems, they are typically tackled by heuristic solvers. Classical solvers such as LKH-3~\cite{lkhtsp,lkhcvrp} and HGS~\cite{hgs} achieve near-optimal performance through decades of manual algorithm engineering, while recent neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) methods~\cite{MLCO,RL4CO,neuopt,2optlearning} aim to learn heuristic rules automatically from data. Despite their promise, the practical adoption of neural routing solvers remains limited by generalization. A solver trained on one distribution should remain effective when directly applied to instances with different scales, structures, and benchmark distributions. However, recent studies show that many neural routing solvers degrade sharply under zero-shot evaluation, and some even fall behind simple construction heuristics such as nearest neighbor and random insertion~\cite{nrs_survey}.

Learning-guided optimization (LGO)~\cite{learn2branch,learn2delegate,neurolkh} offers a promising path toward more reliable neural solvers by embedding learned signals into classical heuristic frameworks. Instead of replacing the entire solver with an end-to-end neural policy, LGO improves specific algorithmic components while preserving the robustness and scalability of mature solvers~\cite{learn2delegate,deepaco,learn2seg,huang2024contrastive}. Among these methods, learning to guide Ant Colony Optimization (ACO)~\cite{ACO,maxmin} is a representative direction for neural assistance in population search~\cite{deepaco,gtgaco}. ACO constructs candidate solutions through probabilistic transitions biased by heuristic priors and dynamically updated pheromones. Its population mechanism makes it naturally suitable for exploration and adaptation, but current neural ACO methods use this structure only partially.

Most existing neural ACO methods learn static guidance over edges from the input instance: some initialize the heuristic prior~\cite{deepaco,gfacs}, while others learn both heuristic and pheromone-like matrices~\cite{gtgaco}. In all cases, the neural model produces guidance from instance geometry \emph{only once}, and the resulting heatmap is fixed throughout the entire ACO search. This design is mismatched with the solver it guides. During training, the model sees static instance features; during inference, it is deployed inside a process where pheromones, incumbent solutions, and explored regions evolve over many iterations. A static heatmap learned from a narrow source distribution may therefore become unreliable when the search trajectory changes on benchmark instances from different distributions.

To reduce this mismatch, we first propose \textbf{\DyNACO{}} (Dynamic Neural Ant Colony Optimization), a dynamic base model for neural ACO\@. Instead of training a model to emit a single heatmap before search begins, \DyNACO{} trains a policy over the search trajectory. The policy periodically observes the current pheromone distribution and incumbent solution, extracts representations of the optimization state, and emits updated guidance over candidate edges for the next search phase. We formalize this process as a \emph{semi-Markov Decision Process}, where each high-level policy action influences a block of low-level ACO iterations. In this way, neural guidance becomes part of the iterative solver rather than a fixed pre-processing signal.

Then, on top of \DyNACO{}, we propose \textbf{\DyNACS{}} (Dynamic Neural Ant Colony Search) to further strengthen population search. Although dynamic guidance adapts to the ACO state, a single policy still imposes one search bias on all ants. This can be restrictive under zero-shot deployment, where different instances and search stages may require different levels of intensification and diversification. \DyNACS{} addresses this limitation through trajectory-diverse guidance. A shared state encoder produces multiple behavior distributions for different ant groups, and these groups interact through the same incumbent solution and pheromone process. During inference, an online routing rule assigns more ants to behaviors that have recently produced better solutions on the current instance. Thus, \DyNACS{} does not run several independent solvers; it turns a single dynamic colony into a heterogeneous neural search process.

Both \DyNACO{} and \DyNACS{} rely on a scalable ACO environment. To make neural guidance practical on large instances, we use perturbation-based ACO on a sparse $K$-nearest-neighbor graph together with \emph{scope-restricted refinement} (SRR). Each policy action changes a localized part of the incumbent solution, and SRR confines local search to the neighborhood of the affected edges. This keeps the reward tied to the policy action, avoids spending refinement effort on unrelated parts of the tour, and allows the same training and inference framework to scale to very large routing instances. The method sections give the full formulation of the dynamic policy, the trajectory-diverse extension, and the refinement backend.

We evaluate \DyNACS{} under a strict zero-shot protocol for both \TSP{} and \CVRP{}. A single model is trained only on 1K-node synthetic instances and is then tested directly on benchmark datasets with different scales and structures, without retraining, fine-tuning, or tuning for individual instances. The results show consistent gains over the unguided backend and prior neural baselines. Further analyses show that the learned guidance does not simply reinforce short edges from the input geometry; it adapts to the evolving pheromone landscape, suppresses over-reinforced edges when the search begins to stagnate, and maintains complementary behaviors across the ant population.

The contributions of the paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We propose \DyNACO{}, a dynamic neural ACO framework formulated as a semi-MDP, where a policy periodically observes pheromones and incumbent solutions to inject guidance throughout the ACO trajectory.
\item We introduce \DyNACS{}, a trajectory-diverse neural search framework built on \DyNACO{}. It learns complementary search behaviors and routes ants among them online according to their recent utility on the current instance.
\item We design a scalable ACO backend based on perturbations with scope-restricted refinement, preserving stable credit assignment while enabling efficient local convergence on sparse graphs up to the 100,000-node scale.
\item We evaluate generalization by training only on 1K-node instances and testing zero-shot on diverse benchmark datasets, where \DyNACS{} outperforms prior neural baselines under the same protocol.
\end{itemize}
