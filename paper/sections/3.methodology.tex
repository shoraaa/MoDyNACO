\subsection{Static Neural ACO}
\label{sec:static-mismatch}

Neural-guided ACO begins with the transition rule in Eq.~\eqref{eq:aco_transition}: pheromone records search experience, heuristic information supplies a fixed prior, and ants sample feasible successors from the resulting distribution. Existing neural-ACO methods usually insert learning into this rule by predicting one heatmap from the input graph before the ACO loop begins. The learned signal is then held fixed while pheromones, incumbents, and sampled solution distributions evolve.

\DyNACO{} replaces this one-shot heatmap with a state-aware policy that repeatedly observes the ACO state and updates edge guidance during search. The policy receives the current pheromone field and incumbent solution, emits guidance for the next block of ACO iterations, and is trained on the cost trajectory induced by those repeated interactions. Dynamic guidance therefore changes the role of the neural model: it no longer initializes the search once, but participates in the same iterative process that updates pheromones and incumbents.

\subsection{Dynamic Guidance as a Semi-MDP}
\label{sec:mdp}
Prior neural-guided ACO methods~\cite{deepaco,gfacs,gtgaco} treat guidance as a \emph{one-shot prediction}: a network observes the instance graph and emits a static heatmap, trained from a single pheromone-free sample of ants, after which the solver runs a full ACO loop with pheromone dynamics to completion without further interaction. The learned signal is therefore trained without the state variables that later govern the search.
\DyNACO{} formalizes dynamic guidance as a two-level hierarchy in which a neural \emph{meta-policy} $\pi_\theta$ repeatedly observes the macro-state and emits updated guidance, while the ACO backend executes $S$ iterations between observations. The policy follows the evolving ACO state rather than a fixed inference horizon, so it can be evaluated under longer ACO budgets by continuing to observe the current macro-state. This semi-MDP formulation enables:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Amortized policy updates.} The neural policy outputs actions only once every $S$ iterations and remains fixed during this interval. This amortizes overhead and effectively reduces variance during policy-gradient training.
\item \emph{Efficient PPO replay.} By framing the guidance process as a semi-MDP, we avoid tracking low-level internal ACO ant steps. To compute PPO importance ratios for off-policy reinforcement learning updates, we only need to store the macro-states at the update steps: the pheromone snapshots, incumbent features, and action traces.
\item \emph{Trajectory-aware objective.} Instead of a myopic single-step reward as used in the literature, we minimize the long-term cumulative cost $\sum_{h=1}^H C_h$ across the full MDP trajectory.
\end{enumerate}

Formally, we model this as a \emph{semi-MDP with fixed-duration macro-actions} as follows:
\begin{itemize}[topsep=0pt]
\item \emph{State} $\mathcal{S}_h = (G, \tau^{(h,0)}, b^{(h)})$: At update step $h$, the macro-state comprises the static $K$-nearest-neighbor candidate graph $G$, the pheromone matrix $\tau^{(h,0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$ at the start of the interval (indexed as $h,0$), and the current solution $b^{(h)}$.
\item \emph{Action} $a_h = z_\theta(\mathcal{S}_h) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$: A residual logit matrix emitted by the \DyNACO{} policy that shapes the pheromone matrix.
\item \emph{Transition} $P(\mathcal{S}_{h+1} \mid \mathcal{S}_h, a_h)$: The environment executes $S$ internal iterations of ACO (comprising ant sampling, scope-restricted refinement, and pheromone updates), functioning as a stochastic, state-dependent transition kernel.
\item \emph{Cost} $C_h = \frac{1}{S \cdot m} \sum_{s=1}^{S} \sum_{a=1}^{m} C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s})$: The average post-refinement tour cost across all $m$ ants over the $S$ iterations. Our objective is to minimize the expected cumulative cost $\sum_{h=1}^H C_h$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{State Representation: Geometry, Pheromone, and Incumbent}
\label{sec:agent}


\label{sec:state}
Prior neural-ACO methods condition solely on \emph{static} instance geometry, yielding guidance that cannot adapt to the solver's evolving state. The semi-MDP state $\mathcal{S}_h = (G,\, \tau^{(h,0)},\, b^{(h)})$ defined in Section~\ref{sec:mdp} isolates the two quantities that change between guidance updates: the pheromone field~$\tau$ and the incumbent solution~$b$. Conditioning on these ACO signals makes the guidance dynamic. We retain the static node features used by DeepACO~\cite{deepaco} for each problem and add three sparse edge channels on the $K$-NN graph: scale-normalized distance, local pheromone concentration, and an incumbent-edge indicator. The dynamic channels are recomputed at every outer step, remain bounded by $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot K)$, and are processed by a 12-layer GNN encoder~\cite{deepaco} with interleaved node and edge message passing. Appendix~\ref{app:features} gives the exact feature definitions.

The \DyNACO{} policy outputs a scalar guidance score $z_{ij}$ for each candidate edge via a 3-layer MLP decoder.
The shaped transition kernel adds these logits in log-space (Eq.~\ref{eq:held-logits}), corresponding to a KL-regularized policy where $p^\theta(\cdot\mid i) \propto q(j\mid i)\exp(z_{ij})$.
Because $\tau_{\min} > 0$ and logits remain finite, every candidate retains positive probability; the policy redistributes mass but cannot exclude edges.
\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PPO training for dynamic \DyNACO{}.}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\theta$ (policy params), $E$ (epochs), $H$ (outer steps), $S$ (inner steps), $K_{\text{PPO}}$ (PPO epochs), $m$ (ants)
\FOR{$e = 1 \to E$}
    \STATE $x \sim \mathcal{X}$; \, $\tau^{(1,0)} \gets \tau_{\max}$; \, $b^{(1)} \gets \textsc{Greedy}(x)$
    \FOR{$h = 1 \to H$}
        \STATE $z \gets z_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\mathcal{S}_h)$; \, $\mathcal{D}_h \gets \emptyset$
            \COMMENT{Outer: emit guidance}
        \FOR{$s = 1 \to S$}
            \STATE $\{\pi_{a,s}\}_{a=1}^m \gets \textsc{Sample}(p^\theta, \tau^{(h,s)}, z, b^{(h)})$
                \COMMENT{Inner: ACO sampling}
            \STATE $\mathcal{D}_h \gets \mathcal{D}_h \cup \{(\tau^{(h,s)}, \{\pi_{a,s}\}, \{C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s})\})\}$
            \STATE $a^* \gets \argmin_a C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s})$; \, $b^{(h)} \gets \min(b^{(h)}, \hat{\pi}_{a^*,s})$
            \STATE $\tau^{(h,s+1)} \gets (1{-}\rho)\tau^{(h,s)} + \rho \cdot \Delta\tau(b^{(h)})$
        \ENDFOR
        \STATE $\tau^{(h+1,0)} \gets \tau^{(h,S)}$; \, $b^{(h+1)} \gets b^{(h)}$
        \FOR{$k = 1 \to K_{\text{PPO}}$}
            \STATE $\theta \gets \theta - \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^{\text{PPO}}(\theta; \mathcal{D}_h, z_\theta(\mathcal{S}_h))$
                \COMMENT{PPO update}
        \ENDFOR
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}



\subsection{Trajectory-Aware Training}
\label{sec:training}
Prior methods~\cite{deepaco,gfacs,gtgaco} optimize a \emph{single-step} objective: $\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{\pi \sim p_\theta(\cdot \mid G)} [ C(\pi) ]$, where ants sample once from the neural heuristic alone.
At inference, however, the same static heatmap is inserted into a full ACO loop where pheromone fields evolve over hundreds of iterations.
This discrepancy precludes learning adaptive behavior: because training never exposes the model to pheromone dynamics, the heatmap inevitably conflicts with the pheromone landscape in later iterations.

We instead optimize the expected per-iteration post-SRR cost across the full trajectory.
Let $\hat{\pi}_{a,s} = \mathrm{LS}(\pi_{a,s})$ denote the tour produced by ant $a$ at sampling iteration $s$ after local search:
\begin{equation}
J(\theta) = \mathbb{E} \left[ \frac{1}{I} \sum_{h=1}^H \sum_{s=1}^S \frac{1}{m} \sum_{a=1}^m C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s}) \right].
\end{equation}
This \emph{trajectory-aware} objective averages cost over all $I = H \times S$ iterations, training the policy to remain effective from early exploration through late exploitation and optimizing the full cost trajectory instead of a single endpoint.

Under perturbative sampling each ant modifies at most $M$ edges, and the log-probability of its trajectory is the sum over stochastic edge choices.
Because the number of stochastic decisions $T_a$ varies across ants (deterministic steps with a single feasible candidate contribute $\log p = 0$), we normalize by decision count: $\bar{\ell}_{a,s} = \frac{1}{T_a} \sum_{t=1}^{T_a} \log p^\theta(v_t \mid u_t)$.
This per-decision normalization prevents ants with more stochastic steps from dominating the gradient.
We compute a per-iteration baseline $b_s = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{a=1}^{m} C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s})$ and define advantage as $A_{a,s} = b_s - C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s})$, so that better-than-average tours receive positive advantage.
For PPO we normalize advantages across the batch: $\hat{A}_{a,s} = (A_{a,s} - \mu_A) / (\sigma_A + \epsilon)$.

We optimize using PPO with trajectory replay.
After collecting trajectories for $S$ sampling iterations under $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$, we perform $K_{\text{PPO}}$ optimization epochs using the clipped surrogate:
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{PPO}}(\theta)
= -\mathbb{E}_{a,s}\Big[\min\big(
 r_{a,s}(\theta)\hat{A}_{a,s},\\
\operatorname{clip}\!\left(r_{a,s}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon\right)
\hat{A}_{a,s}\big)\Big].
\end{multline}
where $r_{a,s}(\theta) = \pi_\theta(\pi_{a,s} | \mathcal{S}_h) / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\pi_{a,s} | \mathcal{S}_h)$, $\epsilon=0.1$, and $\hat{A}_{a,s}$ is the normalized advantage.
Replay remains memory-efficient at the macro-step level. For each outer step, we store the incumbent successor array needed to reconstruct incumbent-derived edge features. For each inner iteration, we store the sparse pheromone snapshot, the stochastic action trace of each ant, the feasible-candidate mask at each stochastic decision, and the post-refinement costs. These quantities are sufficient to recompute PPO log-probabilities under the current policy because the transition probability depends only on the shaped kernel and the stored feasible set; deterministic steps with a single feasible candidate contribute zero log-probability. \DyNACO{} can therefore recompute importance ratios without storing the full GNN computation graph, while all state features remain on the sparse $K$-NN graph with $\mathcal{O}(nK)$ memory.
The full procedure for the dynamic base model is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:train}. With \DyNACO{}, guidance changes with pheromone and incumbent information instead of remaining fixed from initialization. Within each macro-step, however, all ants still follow the same learned edge preference. \DyNACS{} keeps the same dynamic state representation and lets different ant groups follow complementary state-conditioned priors.


\section{Trajectory-Diverse Neural Ant Colony Search}
\label{sec:multi-trajectory}

\DyNACO{} makes neural guidance responsive to the current ACO state, but the policy still emits one guidance field for the whole colony. All ants therefore share the same learned ordering of candidate perturbations at each macro-step, even though a population-based search process may need several useful continuations from the same pheromone state. The limitation becomes more pronounced after pheromone has concentrated around a narrow region or when an out-of-distribution instance presents several plausible search modes.

\DyNACS{} introduces trajectory diversity within the dynamic colony. From the same encoded macro-state, the policy produces several behavior-conditioned guidance fields and assigns ant groups to them. The groups sample different perturbation trajectories, pass through the same refinement operator, and write successful perturbations back to the shared incumbent solution and pheromone field. A learned allocation policy assigns search effort across behaviors from the current state, allowing the colony to change its composition while preserving one shared search memory.

\subsection{Behavior-Conditioned Ant Groups}
\label{sec:tdg-formulation}

We extend the semi-MDP in Section~\ref{sec:mdp} by augmenting dynamic guidance with a discrete behavior index. At macro-step $h$, the state remains
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_h=(G,\tau^{(h,0)},b^{(h)}),
\end{equation}
but the policy action is now a set of $R$ behavior-conditioned guidance fields,
\begin{equation}
a_h = \{z_\theta^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h)\}_{r=1}^{R},
\qquad
z_\theta^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}.
\end{equation}
Each behavior $r$ defines a trajectory distribution for ants assigned to that behavior:
\begin{equation}
\log p_r^\theta(j \mid i; h, s)
= \alpha \log \tau^{(h,s)}_{ij}
+ \beta \log \eta_{ij}
+ \gamma z_{ij}^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h).
\label{eq:multi-trajectory-kernel}
\end{equation}
The $R$ behavior-conditioned distributions define perturbation trajectories inside one shared solver state, preserving the same macro-state, incumbent solution, pheromone update, and SRR projection.

Let $\mathcal{A}_r^{(h,s)}$ be the ants assigned to behavior $r$ at inner iteration $s$. The transition cost for a macro-step is still measured over the combined population,
\begin{equation}
C_h =
\frac{1}{S m}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{r=1}^{R}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}_r^{(h,s)}} C(\hat{\pi}_{a,s}),
\end{equation}
but each trajectory log-probability is evaluated under its assigned behavior,
\begin{equation}
\log \pi_\theta(\pi_{a,s}\mid r,\mathcal{S}_h)
= \sum_{t=1}^{T_a}\log p_r^\theta(v_t\mid u_t;h,s),
\end{equation}
where the behavior index is fixed by the allocation policy for that ant group. Neural guidance is thereby represented as a mixture of trajectory distributions: each behavior is evaluated through the quality of its refined trajectories, while all behaviors cooperate through the shared colony state.

\subsection{Behavior-Conditioned Guidance Generation}
\label{sec:tdg-policy}

The mixture model shares the state representation used by \DyNACO{} and learns multiple behavior-conditioned guidance maps on top of that representation. Let
\begin{equation}
\{e_{ij}^{(h)}\}_{(i,j)\in G}
=
\operatorname{GNN}_{\theta}
\bigl(G,\tau^{(h,0)},b^{(h)}\bigr)
\end{equation}
denote the sparse edge embeddings produced from the current macro-state. A behavior-conditioned decoder family maps these embeddings to $R$ sparse guidance fields:
\begin{equation}
z_{ij}^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h)
= D_{\theta,r}(e_{ij}^{(h)}),
\qquad r=1,\ldots,R,
\label{eq:multi-trajectory-logit}
\end{equation}
Because all behavior-conditioned distributions are grounded in the same encoded search state, their differences reflect behavior-specific edge-ordering preferences under a common colony state. The mixture factorizes one dynamic policy into a shared search representation and multiple behavior-conditioned guidance maps.

We use low-rank behavior conditioning to keep this policy family lightweight. For decoder layer $\ell$, behavior $r$ uses
\begin{equation}
W_{\ell}^{(r)}
= W_{\ell}
+ \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}}{d_{\mathrm{L}}}B_{\ell,r}A_{\ell,r},
\qquad
h_{\ell}^{(r)}
= \sigma\!\left(W_{\ell}^{(r)}h_{\ell-1}^{(r)}+b_{\ell}\right),
\label{eq:behavior-decoder}
\end{equation}
where $W_{\ell}$ and $b_{\ell}$ are shared, $A_{\ell,r}$ and $B_{\ell,r}$ are behavior-specific low-rank factors, $d_{\mathrm{L}}$ is the rank, and $\alpha_{\mathrm{L}}$ controls the scale of the deviation. The same form is used for the output transformation that produces the scalar edge logit. Applying behavior conditioning inside the decoder lets different search modes transform the shared edge representation before ranking candidate perturbations, so intensifying and diversifying moves can emerge from the same pheromone state. Low-rank deviations provide this distributed conditioning while retaining shared statistical strength and keeping the additional cost small relative to the shared GNN encoder. The resulting output is a sparse tensor in $\mathbb{R}^{R\times N\times K}$, matching the candidate graph used by the ACO backend.

\subsection{Shared Pheromone and Incumbent Memory}
\label{sec:tdg-shared-colony}

At each inner ACO iteration, the ant population is partitioned into groups $\{\mathcal{A}_r\}_{r=1}^R$. Ants in group $r$ sample perturbation trajectories under $p_r^\theta$, but all groups start from the same reference solution and contribute to the same post-SRR candidate pool. The best refined trajectory among the combined population updates the incumbent, and the shared pheromone field is updated from that incumbent. The next macro-state aggregates the effect of all behavior-conditioned distributions:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{h+1}
=
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{ACO}}
\bigl(\mathcal{S}_h,\{z_\theta^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h)\}_{r=1}^{R}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The shared transition couples the behavior-conditioned distributions at every inner iteration. A useful perturbation found by one behavior reinforces the common colony state, and the other behaviors later respond through the same pheromone field and incumbent solution.

\subsection{Learned Behavior Allocation}
\label{sec:tdg-allocation}

Trajectory-diverse guidance also needs a rule for assigning ant mass across behaviors. We learn a state-conditioned allocator from the same encoded macro-state used to produce the guidance fields. Let
\begin{equation}
\bar e^{(h)}
=
\frac{1}{|G|}
\sum_{(i,j)\in G} e_{ij}^{(h)}
\end{equation}
be a pooled summary of the current sparse edge representation. The allocator emits behavior logits
\begin{equation}
q^{(h)} = a_{\theta}(\bar e^{(h)}),
\qquad
\omega_r^{(h)}
=
\frac{\exp(q_r^{(h)}/\lambda)}
{\sum_{r'=1}^{R}\exp(q_{r'}^{(h)}/\lambda)},
\label{eq:learned-allocation}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda$ is the allocation temperature. The weights $\omega^{(h)}$ define the population composition used by the shared colony. Given $m$ ants, each active behavior receives a small floor allocation and the remaining ants are assigned according to $\omega^{(h)}$:
\begin{multline}
m_r^{(h)}
= m_{\min}+\tilde m_r^{(h)},
\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^{R}m_r^{(h)}=m, \\
\qquad
\tilde m^{(h)} \sim
\operatorname{Multinomial}\!\left(m-Rm_{\min},\omega^{(h)}\right).
\label{eq:learned-ant-counts}
\end{multline}
At deployment, these weights determine how many ants follow each behavior in the mixed colony. Search effort moves with the encoded ACO state while all behaviors continue to share one pheromone field and one local-refinement procedure. The floor allocation keeps each behavior represented, and the learned weights move additional ants toward behaviors favored by the current state.

\subsection{Training and Inference}
\label{sec:tdg-training}

\DyNACS{} is trained with the same trajectory-aware PPO procedure as \DyNACO{}, with trajectory likelihoods conditioned on the assigned behavior and allocation updates derived from the same refined rollout outcomes. During training, the mixture discovers complementary search modes through the shared colony. All behaviors affect the transition, and the guidance update is taken from the behavior group that produced the strongest refined trajectories in the rollout. Let $s_r^{(h)}$ be the refined-cost score of behavior $r$ at macro-step $h$, computed from the ants assigned to that behavior, with larger values denoting better refined trajectories. The selected behavior is
\begin{equation}
r_h^\star = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}} s_r^{(h)}
\end{equation}
which denotes the behavior with the best refined trajectory score at macro-step $h$. For an ant assigned to this behavior, the PPO ratio is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}(\theta)
=
\exp\!\left(
\bar{\ell}_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}(\theta)
-\bar{\ell}_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
\right),
\end{equation}
where $\bar{\ell}_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}$ is the per-decision normalized log-probability under $p_{r_h^\star}^\theta$. The clipped objective is applied to the selected behavior-conditioned trajectories:
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TD}}(\theta)
= -\mathbb{E}_{a\in\mathcal{A}_{r_h^\star},s}\Big[
\min\big(
\rho_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}(\theta)\hat{A}_{a,s},\\
\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{a,s}^{(r_h^\star)}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
\hat{A}_{a,s}\big)\Big].
\end{multline}
All behaviors remain part of the search transition because their refined trajectories can update the shared incumbent and pheromone field. The allocation weights are learned from the same macro-state representation used for guidance. If $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)}$ is the reward assigned to the sampled allocation, using the best refined cost in the mixed population, its clipped objective is
\begin{multline}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{alloc}}(\theta)
= -\mathbb{E}_{h}\Big[
\min\big(
\rho_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)}(\theta)\hat A_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)},\\
\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)}(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)
\hat A_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)}\big)\Big]
-\lambda_{\mathrm{ent}}\mathcal{H}(\omega^{(h)}),
\end{multline}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{alloc}}^{(h)}$ is the likelihood ratio of the sampled allocation under the new and old allocator policies. The full update combines behavior-conditioned trajectory learning with allocation learning and, optionally, a small information-theoretic diversity regularizer that maximizes the Jensen divergence among the row-wise transition distributions induced by $\{z^{(r)}\}_{r=1}^R$. The regularizer keeps behavior distributions separated while the optimization objective remains the refined trajectory cost.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Trajectory-diverse guidance with learned behavior allocation for \DyNACS{}.}
\label{alg:trajectory-diverse}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\theta$ (shared encoder, behavior-conditioned decoder, allocator), $R$ (behaviors), $m$ (ants), $H$ (outer steps), $S$ (inner steps)
\STATE Initialize $\tau^{(1,0)} \gets \tau_{\max}$ and $b^{(1)} \gets \textsc{Greedy}(x)$
\FOR{$h = 1 \to H$}
    \STATE Encode $\mathcal{S}_h=(G,\tau^{(h,0)},b^{(h)})$
    \STATE Emit behavior-conditioned guidance fields $\{z_\theta^{(r)}(\mathcal{S}_h)\}_{r=1}^R$ and allocation weights $\omega^{(h)}$
    \STATE Assign ant counts $\{m_r^{(h)}\}_{r=1}^R$ from the learned allocation policy
    \FOR{$s = 1 \to S$}
        \FOR{$r = 1 \to R$}
            \STATE Sample $m_r^{(h)}$ perturbation trajectories under $p_r^\theta(\cdot \mid \mathcal{S}_h,\tau^{(h,s)})$
        \ENDFOR
        \STATE Apply SRR to all sampled trajectories
        \STATE Update $b^{(h)}$ with the best refined trajectory from the combined population
        \STATE Update $\tau^{(h,s+1)}$ using the shared incumbent
        \STATE Record refined costs for trajectory and allocation-policy updates during training
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $\tau^{(h+1,0)} \gets \tau^{(h,S)}$; \, $b^{(h+1)} \gets b^{(h)}$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

After \DyNACS{} assigns ant groups to behavior-conditioned priors, the sampled actions must remain local, cheap, and trainable on large routing instances. The search backend provides this execution layer by turning \DyNACS{} guidance into sparse perturbation and refinement steps.

\section{Scalable Search Backend}
\label{sec:computational-alignment}
\label{sec:backend}

The previous two sections define what the policy observes and how one or several guidance fields shape search behavior. We now describe the backend that runs this policy at large scale. The backend is shared by \DyNACO{} and \DyNACS{}: it stores search state on a sparse candidate graph, converts edge-level guidance into short stochastic perturbation trajectories, and projects those trajectories back to locally consistent solutions before rewards are assigned. Its role is to keep the neural decision horizon short enough for policy learning while preserving the refinement quality expected from ACO\@.

\subsection{Sparse Candidate Graph}
\label{sec:sparse-candidate-graph}

All neural inference, pheromone storage, and action tracing are performed on a sparse $K$-nearest-neighbor candidate graph. The graph supplies the feasible candidate list used by the transition kernel and bounds the neural output to $\mathbb{R}^{N\times K}$ for \DyNACO{} and $\mathbb{R}^{R\times N\times K}$ for \DyNACS{}. Sparse graph storage lets the learned state representation use the same edge set as the ACO backend and avoids dense heatmap storage on large instances.

\subsection{Perturbation-Based ACO}
\label{sec:perturbation-aco}

In prior neural-guided ACO frameworks~\cite{deepaco,gfacs,gtgaco}, each ant constructs a tour from scratch. Full construction makes the policy responsible for a sequence of $N$ edge decisions, which becomes expensive at large scales and exposes learning to sequential error accumulation. It also leaves no explicit reference state around which a post-processing step can preserve credit assignment. We instead use the current incumbent as a reference trajectory and let each ant modify only a small subset of candidate edges on the sparse candidate graph.

At sampling iteration $s$ of guidance update $h$, transition probabilities combine pheromones, heuristics, and neural guidance via a \emph{guidance weight} $\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
\log p^\theta(j \mid i; h, s) = \alpha \log \tau^{(h,s)}_{ij} + \beta \log \eta_{ij} + \gamma\, z_{ij}(\mathcal{S}_h).
\label{eq:held-logits}
\end{equation}
During training $\gamma{=}1$ (constant forcing); at inference $\gamma$ may be annealed (see Section~\ref{sec:inference}). The policy outputs $z_\theta(\mathcal{S}_h)$ once per guidance update and holds it constant for $S$ sampling iterations, decoupling neural guidance (updated at guidance-update boundaries) from pheromone dynamics (updated at every sampling iteration). Crucially, prior neural-ACO methods~\cite{deepaco,gfacs,gtgaco} replace the heuristic term~$\eta_{ij}$ with neural output, discarding hand-crafted domain knowledge. We instead retain both $\tau_{ij}$ and $\eta_{ij}$, and introduce neural logits~$z_{ij}$ as a third, additive term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:held-logits}. This preserves the domain knowledge encoded in $\eta$ while letting the network provide complementary, state-dependent guidance.

Formally, each ant initializes from the incumbent solution $b^{(h)}$ and executes up to $M$ stochastic edge interventions. At each intervention, the ant samples a successor node $v$ from the feasible candidate set according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:held-logits}; edges $(u,v)$ absent from the incumbent are recorded as anchors for the subsequent scope-restricted refinement mechanism. The trajectory log-probability decomposes over stochastic decisions:
\begin{equation}
\log \pi_\theta(\pi_{a,s} \mid \mathcal{S}_h) = \sum_{t=1}^{T_a} \log p^\theta(v_t \mid u_t; h, s),
\label{eq:logp-trajectory}
\end{equation}
where $T_a \le M$ denotes the number of stochastic interventions, i.e., steps at which multiple candidates were feasible. The learning problem changes from constructing all $N$ tour edges to selecting a small intervention set of size at most $M \ll N$. The decision horizon is therefore bounded by $M$ rather than $N$, which reduces sequential error accumulation and stabilizes policy learning at scale. The localized intervention set also defines the region on which refinement should be allowed to affect the reward.

\subsection{Scope-Restricted Refinement}
\label{sec:localized-ls}
In neural-guided ACO~\cite{deepaco,gfacs,gtgaco}, refinement is part of the training environment: the post-refinement cost is the reward signal used for policy-gradient updates. Refinement therefore creates a projection-credit trade-off. If refinement is too weak, sampled trajectories remain under-refined and rewards are noisy. If refinement is too strong and unconstrained, it can rewrite most of the sampled intervention, so the final cost reflects the refinement heuristic instead of the neural decision. We refer to this failure mode as \emph{gradient washout}.

Figure~\ref{fig:credit_assignment} illustrates this trade-off using DeepACO on TSP-200 under three refinement strategies. With \emph{Full 2-opt} (red), the policy fails to converge because the local search heavily rewrites the sampled tour and severs the causal link between actions and rewards. \emph{Limited 2-opt} (orange) preserves more of the learning signal by stopping after $N/4$ swaps, but this early stopping bottlenecks solution quality. \emph{Neural Local Search}~\cite{deepaco} (blue) improves quality by using the neural policy to score candidate swaps, but it is not scalable because it requires neural evaluations inside the refinement loop.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/nls_vs_limited_vs_unconstrained.pdf}
    \caption{Credit-assignment trade-off under different local-search strategies when training DeepACO on TSP-200.}
    \label{fig:credit_assignment}
\end{figure}

Scalability imposes an additional constraint. Standard 2-opt costs $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ per tour, which is prohibitive when refinement must be applied to every ant at every iteration. Classical large-scale ACO methods~\cite{partialACO, P-ACO} reduce this cost by applying local search only occasionally or only to elite ants. Policy-gradient training instead requires post-refinement costs for all sampled ants, because every ant contributes an advantage estimate. The refinement operator must therefore preserve the causal link between policy actions and rewards, refine tours to convergence rather than stop at an arbitrary budget, and have cost independent of the full instance size.

Scope-Restricted Refinement (SRR), inspired by~\cite{faco}, provides these properties. Perturbation-based ants start from an incumbent solution that is already locally optimized under the candidate graph. Since an ant modifies only a small set of edges, any newly introduced sub-optimality is localized around those interventions. SRR therefore acts as a local projection operator: it initializes a checklist with the endpoints of the perturbed edges and repeatedly applies improving candidate-graph refinement moves involving checked nodes, reactivating affected neighbors after each accepted move. Conceptually, SRR propagates repair outward from the intervention region until no improving move remains within its scope.

Specifically, SRR provides the three properties required for training. First, because refinement is restricted to the neighborhood of the policy's perturbations, the post-refinement cost remains causally tied to the sampled actions, preserving credit assignment. Second, unlike truncated local search, SRR runs to convergence within its scope; assuming the incumbent was already candidate-graph 2-opt optimal, this restores candidate-graph 2-opt optimality after the perturbation. Third, the refinement cost scales with the perturbation size, $\mathcal{O}(M \cdot K)$, rather than the instance size, $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$, allowing SRR to be applied to every ant at every iteration even at $N{=}100$K.

\subsection{Instantiation for \TSP{} and \CVRP{}}
\label{sec:routing-instantiation}

The backend above is problem-generic at the level of state-conditioned perturbation and local projection. For \TSP{}, perturbation follows~\cite{faco}: ants start from the incumbent tour, add at most $M$ candidate edges under the shaped transition kernel, and SRR applies candidate-limited 2-opt around the affected endpoints. For \CVRP{}, the same procedure uses capacity-aware feasibility masks, depot handling, and a multi-route incumbent representation. The local projection uses capacity-preserving relocate, swap, and 2-opt* moves, again restricted to the neighborhood activated by the sampled perturbations. Appendix~\ref{app:setup} gives the implementation details for both problem families.

\subsection{Stabilized Pheromone Dynamics}
\label{sec:smoothed-mmas}
\MMAS{}~\cite{maxmin} bounds pheromones to $[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]$ to prevent premature convergence, but its cost-dependent bounds create non-stationary input distributions for the state-aware policy. We adopt fixed bounds ($\tau_{\max}{=}1$, $\tau_{\min}{=}1/K$) and update via convex interpolation:
\begin{equation}
\tau^{(h,s+1)}_{ij} \leftarrow (1-\rho)\tau^{(h,s)}_{ij} + \rho \cdot \mathcal{T}_{ij},
\label{eq:smoothed-mmas}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{ij} = \tau_{\max}$ if $(i,j)$ is in the best solution, else $\tau_{\min}$.
The update yields scale-invariant state representations and stabilizes training.
Fixed bounds give the policy a stable pheromone channel for dynamic guidance, and the ablations in Section~\ref{sec:ablations} show that this stabilized state representation improves both the unguided backend and \DyNACO{}.

\subsection{Inference Controls}
\label{sec:inference}
We use constant guidance ($\gamma{=}1$) during training, with no annealing or phased scheduling.
At inference, the guidance weight~$\gamma$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:held-logits}) can be modulated via two strategies: \emph{guidance annealing}, which linearly decays $\gamma \to 0$ across inner iterations; and \emph{phased injection}, which withholds neural guidance for the first fraction of outer steps.
The specific configuration varies by problem and scale: for TSP, we apply annealing at all scales and additionally enable phased injection at TSP-1K; for CVRP, we apply phased injection only at longer iteration budgets ($I{\geq}5000$) and never anneal.

\subsection{Complexity and Overhead}
\label{sec:complexity}

The computational cost separates into sparse neural inference and ACO operations based on perturbations. The GNN encoder runs once per outer step on the $K$-NN graph, giving $\mathcal{O}(H L n K d)$ neural cost for $H$ guidance updates, $L$ layers, $n$ nodes, $K$ candidates, and hidden dimension $d$. During the ACO search, each of the $m$ ants performs at most $M$ stochastic interventions, and SRR scans only the affected candidate neighborhoods. Over $T=H S$ inner iterations, this gives $\mathcal{O}(T m M K)$ sampling and local projection cost.

The learned state and replay buffers are also sparse. Pheromone snapshots, edge features, feasible masks, and action traces are stored on the candidate graph, requiring $\mathcal{O}(nK)$ memory rather than $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ dense heatmaps. \DyNACS{} reuses the \DyNACO{} encoder and adds lightweight behavior maps, so neural guidance can remain active during large search runs instead of serving only as an initialization heuristic.
